The Lifecycle of Software Systems: Why Most Code Dies Young

Elena Vasquez
Mar 14, 2026
12 min read

The average software system has a lifespan far shorter than most engineers assume. While we build with optimism — clean architectures, comprehensive tests, thorough documentation — the reality is that the vast majority of codebases are abandoned within a decade of their creation.

This isn't necessarily a failure. Systems outlive their purpose, organizations pivot, and technologies evolve. But the knowledge embedded in these systems — the decisions, the tradeoffs, the reasoning — deserves to survive beyond the code itself.

Philosophy

Built for the next fifty years,
not the next fifty minutes

FOSSil is designed around a set of convictions about what publishing software should be — and what it has no business being.

01

Built for Permanence

Every design decision is filtered through a single question: will this still work in twenty years? We choose stable formats, simple architectures, and minimal dependencies.

02

Open Formats

Your content lives in plain Markdown, stored in standard file structures. No proprietary databases, no lock-in. Export everything at any time with a single command.

03

You Own Your Writing

Self-hosted by default. Your data, your server, your rules. We provide the software — you keep the sovereignty that platforms quietly take away.

04

No Algorithmic Feeds

There is no engagement metric to optimize. Content is organized by author intent, not machine learning. Readers find writing through structure, not stimulation.

05

Outliving Trends

We don't follow design fads. The interface is calm, typographically sound, and intentionally understated — because the content is what matters.

Product

Every surface, considered

A reading and writing experience designed for focus, clarity, and longevity.

The Paradox of Longevity in Software

Elena Vasquez · Mar 14, 2026 · 12 min read

We build software with the implicit belief that it will endure. We name versions with confidence, plan roadmaps years ahead, and architect systems as if permanence were a technical problem with a technical solution.

But longevity in software is not primarily a technical challenge. It is an organizational one, a cultural one, and — most importantly — an informational one. The code is the easy part.

The question is not whether a system can run in twenty years, but whether anyone will understand why it was built.

What dies first is never the binary. It is the context: the decision rationale, the constraints that shaped the architecture, the assumptions that no longer hold but were never documented.

Content Model

Three types of content,
one coherent system

Every piece of writing in FOSSil has a clear type and place. No ambiguity, no miscategorization.

Articles

Long-form, structured writing. Essays, research, technical documentation. The core of your archive.

Collections

Curated groupings of articles. A table of contents with context — the way a real bibliography works.

Posts

Short, informal notes. Observations, links, quick thoughts. Timestamped, but not over-structured.

Articles can belong to multiple collections. Posts stand alone. Everything is searchable.

Features

What's under the surface

No feature bloat. No dashboards full of metrics. Just the tools that matter for serious publishing.

Markdown-Native Editor

Write in Markdown with a live preview. No WYSIWYG abstractions — your content stays clean and portable from the first keystroke.

Plain-Text Storage

Every article is a .md file on disk. Every collection is a simple directory structure. Your archive is always accessible, even without FOSSil running.

Full-Text Search

Instant search across every article, post, and collection. Results ranked by relevance, not recency or popularity.

Self-Hosted by Default

Deploy on your own server with a single binary. No cloud dependency, no subscription, no third-party data access.

Git Integration

Every change is versioned. Full history, diffs, and the ability to revert to any previous state. Your writing workflow, versioned.

Typographically Sound

Reading experience designed around line length, leading, and type scale — not decoration. Content is always the hero.
